Water Resources

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 3 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Water Scarcity and its Causes

Even though three-fourths of the earth's surface is covered with water, freshwater makes up only a tiny fraction (approx 2.5%).

Water Scarcity: The shortage of water compared to its demand.

  • Quantitative causes: Over-exploitation, excessive use in agriculture (tubewells/pumps), growing population, and industrialization.
  • Qualitative causes: Water is available, but polluted by domestic/industrial wastes, chemicals, pesticides, and fertilizers.

2. Multi-Purpose River Projects (Dams)

Dams were traditionally built to impound rivers and rainwater to use for irrigation. Today, they are built for electricity generation, water supply, flood control, recreation, inland navigation, and fish breeding. Jawaharlal Nehru proudly proclaimed the dams as the "temples of modern India."

Advantages: Generates hydroelectricity, provides irrigation in dry seasons, controls floods in certain areas.
Disadvantages: Displaces local communities, disrupts the natural flow of rivers (poor sediment flow), destroys local flora/fauna, triggers earthquakes.

Social Movements: Mega projects have triggered resistance, e.g., the Narmada Bachao Andolan (Save Narmada Movement) against the Sardar Sarovar Dam.

3. Rainwater Harvesting (RWH)

A viable alternative to multi-purpose projects. Capturing and storing rainwater for future use.

Fig 3.1: Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting mechanism
  • Guls / Kuls: Diversion channels built in hilly/mountainous regions (Western Himalayas) for agriculture.
  • Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting: Very common in Rajasthan (Bikaner, Phalodi, Barmer) to store drinking water in large underground tanks (tankas). Rain falling on sloping roofs is piped into these underground tankas.
  • Inundation Channels: In the flood plains of Bengal to irrigate fields.
  • Khadins / Johads: Agricultural fields converted into rain-fed storage structures in arid/semi-arid regions (Rajasthan) to moisten the soil.
