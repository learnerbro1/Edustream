Political Parties

CBSE Class 10 • Political Science — Chapter 4 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Meaning and Components

Political Party: A group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on some policies and programmes for the society with a view to promote the collective good.

Components: The leaders, the active members, and the followers.

2. Functions of Political Parties

  • Contest Elections: In most democracies, elections are fought mainly among candidates put up by political parties.
  • Policies and Programmes: Parties put forward different policies and voters choose from them.
  • Making Laws: Parties play a decisive role in making laws for a country.
  • Form and Run Government: Big policy decisions are taken by political executive that comes from political parties.
  • Role of Opposition: Losing parties play the role of opposition by voicing different views and criticizing the govt.
  • Shape Public Opinion: They raise and highlight issues.
  • Access to Govt Machinery: They provide people access to government machinery and welfare schemes.

3. Party Systems

One-Party System: Only one party is allowed to control and run the govt (e.g., China).
Two-Party System: Power usually changes between two main parties (e.g., USA, UK).

Multi-Party System: Several parties compete for power (e.g., India). Sometimes, several parties join hands in an alliance or front.

4. National vs State Parties

The Election Commission of India registers political parties. It offers some special facilities to large and established parties, calling them 'Recognised Political Parties'.

  • State Party: Must secure at least 6% of valid votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly of a State and win at least 2 seats.
  • National Party: Must secure at least 6% of total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four States and win at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha. (Examples: INC, BJP, BSP, CPI-M, CPI, NCP, AITC).

5. Challenges and Reforms

Challenges: 1. Lack of internal democracy. 2. Dynastic succession. 3. Growing role of money and muscle power. 4. Absence of meaningful choice to voters.

Reforms made: Anti-defection law (MPs/MLAs lose seat if they change party); Supreme Court order to reduce the influence of money and criminals (mandatory affidavit); Election Commission order to hold organisational elections and file IT returns.
