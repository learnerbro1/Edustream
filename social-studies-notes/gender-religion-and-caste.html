Gender, Religion and Caste

CBSE Class 10 • Political Science — Chapter 3 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Gender and Politics

The gender division is a form of hierarchical social division seen everywhere, but rarely recognized in the study of politics. It is based on social expectations and stereotypes, not biology.

  • Sexual division of labour: A system in which all work inside the home is either done by the women of the family or organized by them through domestic helpers.
  • Feminist Movements: Radical women's movements aimed at equality in personal and family life as well.

Women's Political Representation: The proportion of women in legislature has been very low in India. One way to solve this is to make it legally binding to have a fair proportion of women in elected bodies (e.g., 1/3rd seats reserved in Panchayats and Municipalities).

2. Religion, Communalism and Politics

Religion can also play a role in politics. Gandhiji used to say that religion can never be separated from politics. What he meant by religion was not any particular religion but moral values that inform all religions.

Communalism: The belief that religion is the principal basis of social community. It happens when beliefs of one religion are presented as superior to those of other religions, and state power is used to establish the domination of one religious group.

Forms of Communalism in politics: Everyday beliefs, stereotypes, quest for political dominance, mobilization on religious lines, and communal violence/riots.

Secular State: India is a secular state. There is no official religion. The Constitution provides freedom to profess any religion and prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion.

3. Caste and Politics

Unlike gender and religion, caste division is special to India.

  • Caste inequalities: The caste system was based on exclusion and discrimination against 'outcaste' groups. Social reformers like Jotiba Phule, Gandhiji, B.R. Ambedkar, and Periyar Ramaswami Naicker advocated for a casteless society.
  • Caste in Politics: When parties choose candidates, they keep in mind the caste composition of the electorate. Political parties appeal to caste sentiments.
  • Politics in Caste: Politics also influences the caste system by bringing caste identities into the political arena. Caste groups try to become bigger by incorporating neighboring castes. New kinds of caste groups come up (like 'backward' and 'forward' caste groups).
