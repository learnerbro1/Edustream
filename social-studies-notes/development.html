Development

CBSE Class 10 • Economics — Chapter 1 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. What Development Promises

Different people, different goals: People seek things that are most important for them. For a landless rural laborer, it's more days of work and better wages. For a prosperous farmer from Punjab, it's a high family income through higher support prices.

Conflicting goals: What may be development for one may not be development for the other. It may even be destructive for the other (e.g., industrialists want more dams for electricity, but this displaces tribals).

Income and other goals: Besides seeking more income, people also seek things like equal treatment, freedom, security, and respect. In some cases, these may be more important than more income or more consumption because material goods are not all that you need to live.

2. National Development & Comparison

How to compare different countries or states? For comparing countries, their income is considered to be one of the most important attributes.

  • Average Income (Per Capita Income): Total income of the country divided by its total population.
  • World Bank Report: Uses Per Capita Income to classify countries. Countries with high per capita income are called rich/developed countries, while those with less are low-income countries.

Limitation of Average Income: While averages are useful for comparison, they hide disparities. It does not tell us how the income is distributed among people.

3. Income and Other Criteria

When we think of a nation or a region, we must look at other equally important attributes:

  • Infant Mortality Rate (IMR): Number of children that die before the age of one year as a proportion of 1000 live children born in that particular year.
  • Literacy Rate: Proportion of literate population in the 7 and above age group.
  • Net Attendance Ratio: Total number of children of age group 14-15 years attending school as a percentage of total number of children in the same age group.

Example: Kerala has lower per capita income than Haryana, but a much better Infant Mortality Rate and Literacy Rate due to adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities.

4. Human Development Report

Published by UNDP (United Nations Development Programme), it compares countries based on the educational levels of the people, their health status, and per capita income.

Public Facilities: Money in your pocket cannot buy all the goods and services that you may need to live well. Things like a pollution-free environment, unadulterated medicines, and protection from infectious diseases require collective public provision.

5. Sustainability of Development

Development should take place without damaging the environment, and development in the present should not compromise with the needs of the future generations.

  • Groundwater over-exploitation: A major threat to sustainability in agricultural regions.
  • Exhaustion of natural resources: Non-renewable resources (like crude oil) will get exhausted after a few decades of use.
