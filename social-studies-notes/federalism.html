Federalism

CBSE Class 10 • Political Science — Chapter 2 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. What is Federalism?

Federalism: A system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and its constituent units (like states or provinces).

  • Unitary system: Only one level of govt, or subunits are subordinate. Central govt can pass orders to provincial govts.
  • Federal system: Central govt cannot order the state govt to do something. State govt has its own powers and is not answerable to the centre.

Key Features: 1. Two or more levels of govt. 2. Different tiers govern the same citizens but have their own jurisdiction. 3. Jurisdictions are specified in the constitution. 4. Fundamental provisions cannot be unilaterally changed. 5. Courts act as umpires if disputes arise. 6. Sources of revenue are specified.

2. Types of Federations

Coming Together: Independent states come together on their own to form a bigger unit. They retain equal power. Examples: USA, Switzerland, Australia.
Holding Together: A large country decides to divide its power between constituent states and the national government. Examples: India, Spain, Belgium.

3. What makes India a Federal Country?

The Constitution originally provided for a two-tier system of government (Union Govt and State Govts). Later, a third tier (Panchayats and Municipalities) was added. The Constitution clearly provides a threefold distribution of legislative powers:

  • Union List: Subjects of national importance (Defence, Foreign affairs, Banking, Currency). Only Union Govt makes laws.
  • State List: Subjects of state and local importance (Police, Trade, Commerce, Agriculture). Only State Govt makes laws.
  • Concurrent List: Common interest (Education, Forest, Trade Unions, Marriage). Both can make laws. If they conflict, Union law prevails.
  • Residuary Subjects: Subjects that do not fall in any list (e.g., Computer Software). Union Govt has the power to legislate.

4. Decentralisation in India

When power is taken away from Central and State governments and given to local government, it is called decentralisation.

Major step taken in 1992: The Constitution was amended to make the third tier more powerful.

  • Mandatory to hold regular elections to local government bodies.
  • Seats reserved for SC, ST, and OBCs.
  • At least one-third of positions reserved for women.
  • State Election Commission created to conduct local elections.

Rural local govt is popularly known by the name Panchayati Raj (Gram Panchayat → Panchayat Samiti → Zila Parishad). Urban bodies include Municipalities and Municipal Corporations.
