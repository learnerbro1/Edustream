Money and Credit

CBSE Class 10 • Economics — Chapter 3 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Money as a Medium of Exchange

Before money, people used the Barter System (exchanging goods for goods). It required the Double Coincidence of Wants (both parties had to agree to sell and buy each other's commodities).

Money acts as an intermediate: It eliminates the need for double coincidence of wants. Since it serves as a medium in the exchange process, it is called a medium of exchange.

Modern forms of money: Paper notes and coins. They have no use of their own, but are accepted as a medium of exchange because the currency is authorized by the government of the country. In India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issues currency notes on behalf of the central government.

2. Deposits with Banks and Cheques

People deposit their extra cash with the banks by opening a bank account. Banks pay interest on these deposits.

  • Demand Deposits: Since the deposits in the bank accounts can be withdrawn on demand, these deposits are called demand deposits.
  • Cheque: A paper instructing the bank to pay a specific amount from the person's account to the person in whose name the cheque has been issued.

3. Loan Activities of Banks

Banks keep only a small proportion of their deposits as cash (about 15% in India) to pay depositors who might come to withdraw money. They use the major portion of the deposits to extend loans.

How banks earn money: Banks charge a higher interest rate on loans than what they offer on deposits. The difference between what is charged from borrowers and what is paid to depositors is their main source of income.

4. Two Different Credit Situations

Credit (Loan): An agreement in which the lender supplies the borrower with money, goods, or services in return for the promise of future payment.

  • Positive situation: Credit helps a person increase their earnings and they are better off than before (e.g., manufacturer buying raw materials to meet an order).
  • Negative situation (Debt Trap): Credit pushes the borrower into a situation from which recovery is very painful (e.g., a farmer taking a loan for crops, but the crops fail, forcing them to sell land to repay the loan).

5. Terms of Credit

Interest rate, collateral (an asset that the borrower owns and uses as a guarantee until the loan is repaid), and documentation requirement together comprise the terms of credit.

6. Formal vs Informal Sectors of Credit

Formal Sector: Loans from Banks and Cooperatives. Supervised by the RBI. Lower rates of interest. Requires proper documentation and collateral.
Informal Sector: Moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives. No organization supervises them. Very high rates of interest, leading to a debt trap. No collateral needed.

Self Help Groups (SHGs): A group of 15-20 rural poor, especially women, who pool their savings. Members can take small loans from the group itself to meet their needs. Helps them overcome the problem of lack of collateral and saves them from moneylenders.
