The Age of Industrialisation

CBSE Class 10 • History — Chapter 4 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Before the Industrial Revolution

Even before factories began to dot the landscape in England, there was large-scale industrial production for the international market. This phase is known as Proto-industrialisation.

  • Merchants supplied money to peasants and artisans in the countryside to produce goods.
  • It allowed peasants to supplement their shrinking agricultural income.

2. The Coming of the Factory

The earliest factories in England came up by the 1730s. The first symbol of the new era was cotton.

  • Richard Arkwright created the cotton mill, moving production from homes to factories under one roof.
  • Pace of Change: Traditional industries (like food processing, pottery) still employed a large workforce; the new industries did not displace them entirely right away.
Fig 4.1: The Spinning Jenny, which sped up the spinning process

3. Hand Labour and Steam Power

In Victorian Britain, there was no shortage of human labour. Industrialists did not want to introduce machines that required large capital investment when human labor was cheap.

Life of the Workers: Plentiful labor meant low wages. Seasonal work meant long periods of unemployment. Workers protested against machines like the Spinning Jenny because it took away their spinning jobs.

4. Industrialisation in the Colonies (India)

Before the machine age, Indian silk and cotton goods dominated the international market. Surat, Hooghly, and Masulipatnam were key ports.

Weavers under the East India Company

The EIC established a monopoly right to trade. They appointed a paid servant called the Gomastha to supervise weavers, collect supplies, and examine cloth quality. This led to clashes, as Gomasthas often acted arrogantly and cut off weavers from other buyers.

Manchester comes to India

British cotton goods flooded the Indian market. Indian weavers faced two problems: their export market collapsed, and the local market shrank due to cheap Manchester imports.

5. Early Entrepreneurs in India

Many Indian entrepreneurs made wealth from the China trade (opium/tea). Dwarkanath Tagore, Dinshaw Petit, and J.N. Tata set up early cotton and iron/steel mills in India.

European Managing Agencies controlled a large sector of Indian industries till the First World War.
