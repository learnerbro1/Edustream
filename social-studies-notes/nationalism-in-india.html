Nationalism in India

CBSE Class 10 • History — Chapter 2 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. First World War, Khilafat and Non-Cooperation

The First World War created a new economic and political situation (huge defense expenditure, price rise, forced recruitment, crop failure, influenza epidemic).

The Idea of Satyagraha

Mahatma Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915. He introduced Satyagraha: A novel method of mass agitation emphasizing the power of truth and the need to search for truth without physical force.

  • 1916: Champaran (Bihar) - against the oppressive plantation system.
  • 1917: Kheda (Gujarat) - supporting peasants demanding revenue relaxation.
  • 1918: Ahmedabad (Gujarat) - supporting cotton mill workers.
The Rowlatt Act (1919) & Jallianwala Bagh:

The Rowlatt Act gave the government enormous powers to repress political activities and detain political prisoners without trial for 2 years.
On 13th April 1919, General Dyer ordered open fire on a peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh (Amritsar), killing hundreds.

2. Non-Cooperation Movement (NCM)

Adopted at the Nagpur session (1920). Gandhi proposed that Indians should surrender titles, boycott civil services, army, police, courts, legislative councils, schools, and foreign goods.

In Towns: Middle-class participation. Foreign cloth burnt, liquor shops picketed. But slowed down because Khadi was expensive and Indian institutions were slow to come up.
In Countryside: Peasants led by Baba Ramchandra (Awadh) fought against high rents. Tribals in Gudem Hills (Andhra) led by Alluri Sitaram Raju rebelled against forest laws.

Chauri Chaura Incident (1922): A peaceful protest turned violent, leading to the death of 22 policemen. Gandhi immediately called off the NCM.

3. Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM)

In 1929, under Jawaharlal Nehru, the Lahore Congress formalized the demand for 'Purna Swaraj' (Full Independence).

Fig 2.1: Gandhi leading the Salt March from Sabarmati to Dandi

The Salt March (Dandi March)

Gandhi found salt a powerful symbol to unite the nation. On 12th March 1930, he marched 240 miles to Dandi. On 6th April, he ceremonially broke the salt law by boiling seawater. This marked the beginning of the Civil Disobedience Movement.

4. The Sense of Collective Belonging

Nationalism spread through history, fiction, folklore, songs, and prints.

  • Bharat Mata: The image was first created by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay (who also wrote Vande Mataram). Later painted beautifully by Abanindranath Tagore.
  • Folklore: Leaders like Rabindranath Tagore and Natesa Sastri collected folk tales to revive national identity.
  • Flags: Swadeshi movement tricolour (red, green, yellow) and later Gandhi's Swaraj flag (red, green, white with a spinning wheel).
