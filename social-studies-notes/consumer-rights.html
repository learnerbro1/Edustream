Consumer Rights

CBSE Class 10 • Economics — Chapter 5 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. The Consumer in the Marketplace

Consumers participate in the market when they purchase goods and services. However, they are often exploited by producers and traders. Forms of exploitation include:

  • Selling adulterated or defective goods.
  • Weighing less than what they should.
  • Charging more than the Maximum Retail Price (MRP).
  • Providing false information through advertisements.

Because the consumers were scattered and unorganized, a movement was required to protect their interests.

2. Consumer Movement

The consumer movement arose out of dissatisfaction of the consumers. In India, it originated as a 'social force' with the necessity of protecting consumers against unethical trade practices like rampant food shortages, hoarding, black marketing, and adulteration.

COPRA 1986: A major step was taken in 1986 by the Indian government with the enactment of the Consumer Protection Act 1986 (popularly known as COPRA).

3. Rights of a Consumer

Under COPRA, consumers have been given specific rights:

  • Right to Safety: Protection against goods and services that are hazardous to life and property (e.g., defective pressure cookers).
  • Right to be Informed: Right to get information about the quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price of goods (Details given on the packaging like MRP, expiry date, ingredients).
  • Right to Choose: Any consumer who receives a service can choose whether to continue receiving the service or not.
  • Right to Seek Redressal: Right to seek compensation against unfair trade practices and exploitation.
  • Right to Represent (Right to be Heard): COPRA enabled setting up of consumer courts where consumers can present their cases.

4. Taking the Consumer Movement Forward

India is one of the countries that have exclusive courts for consumer redressal.

  • Three-tier quasi-judicial machinery: District Commissions, State Commissions, and National Commission.
  • Consumer Forums / Councils: Voluntary organizations that guide consumers on how to file cases in the consumer court.
  • National Consumers' Day: 24 December (the day COPRA was enacted in 1986).

Challenges: The consumer redressal process is becoming cumbersome, expensive, and time-consuming. Evidence is not always easy to gather, and rules are often ignored.

Consumers need to be conscious of their rights and duties. Look for quality marks like ISI, Agmark, or Hallmark before buying.
