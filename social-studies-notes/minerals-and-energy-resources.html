Minerals and Energy Resources

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 5 • Detailed Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. What is a Mineral?

Geologists define a mineral as a "homogenous, naturally occurring substance with a definable internal structure." They are found in varied forms in nature, ranging from the hardest diamond to the softest talc.

Mode of Occurrence of Minerals

Minerals are usually found in "ores." An ore is an accumulation of any mineral mixed with other elements. Minerals generally occur in the following forms:

  • In Igneous and Metamorphic Rocks: Minerals occur in cracks, crevices, faults, or joints. Smaller occurrences are called veins, and larger ones are called lodes. (e.g., Tin, copper, zinc, lead).
  • In Sedimentary Rocks: They occur in beds or layers formed as a result of deposition and concentration in horizontal strata. (e.g., Coal, iron ore, gypsum).
  • Decomposition of Surface Rocks: Involves the removal of soluble constituents, leaving a residual mass of weathered material containing ores. (e.g., Bauxite).
  • Alluvial Deposits (Placer Deposits): Occur in sands of valley floors and the base of hills. These minerals are not corroded by water. (e.g., Gold, silver, tin, platinum).
  • Ocean Waters: Contain vast quantities of minerals, though most are too widely diffused. (e.g., Common salt, magnesium, bromine).
Rat-Hole Mining: In North-Eastern India (like Meghalaya), most minerals are owned by individuals or communities. Mining is done by family members in the form of a long narrow tunnel, known as 'Rat-Hole' mining. The National Green Tribunal has declared such activities illegal.

2. Classification of Minerals

A. Metallic Minerals

Ferrous Minerals: Account for about three-fourths of the total value of metallic minerals. They contain iron.

  • Iron Ore: The backbone of industrial development.
    - Magnetite: Finest quality (up to 70% iron) with excellent magnetic qualities.
    - Hematite: Most important industrial iron ore (50-60% iron).
    Major Belts: Odisha-Jharkhand belt, Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur belt, Bellary-Chitradurga-Chikkamagaluru-Tumakuru belt (Kudremukh mines in Karnataka are 100% export unit).
  • Manganese: Mainly used in manufacturing steel and ferro-manganese alloys. Bleaching powder, insecticides, and paints also require it. Odisha is the largest producer.

Non-Ferrous Minerals: Do not contain iron but play a vital role in metallurgy, engineering, and electrical industries.

  • Copper: India is critically deficient in copper. Being malleable, ductile, and a good conductor, it is mainly used in electrical cables and electronics. (Balaghat mines in MP, Khetri mines in Rajasthan).
  • Bauxite: Formed by the decomposition of rocks rich in aluminum silicates. Aluminum is extracted from bauxite. It is known for its extreme lightness, good conductivity, and great malleability. (Odisha is the largest producer).

B. Non-Metallic Minerals

  • Mica: Can be clear, black, green, red, yellow, or brown. Due to its excellent di-electric strength, low power loss factor, and resistance to high voltage, mica is indispensable in the electric and electronic industries. (Koderma-Gaya-Hazaribagh belt of Jharkhand).
  • Limestone: Composed of calcium carbonate or calcium and magnesium carbonates. Found in sedimentary rocks. It is the basic raw material for the cement industry and essential for smelting iron ore in the blast furnace.

3. Hazards of Mining & Conservation

Hazards: The dust and noxious fumes inhaled by miners make them vulnerable to pulmonary diseases. The risk of collapsing mine roofs, inundation, and fires in coal mines are constant threats. Water sources get contaminated, and land degradation occurs.
Conservation: Minerals are non-renewable and take millions of years to form. We must use them in a planned and sustainable manner. Use of improved technologies, recycling of metals, and using scrap metals are essential steps.

4. Energy Resources

Energy is required for all activities. Resources are classified into Conventional and Non-Conventional sources.

A. Conventional Sources of Energy

These have been used for a long time and are exhaustible.

  • Coal: Most abundantly available fossil fuel in India. Used for power generation and industry.
    - Peat: Low carbon, high moisture, low heating capacity.
    - Lignite: Low-grade brown coal, soft with high moisture (Neyveli, Tamil Nadu).
    - Bituminous: Most popular coal for commercial use. High grade, deeply buried.
    - Anthracite: Highest quality hard coal.
  • Petroleum: Next major energy source. Provides fuel for heat and lighting, lubricants, and raw materials for manufacturing. Found in fault traps in porous rock layers. (Mumbai High, Gujarat, Assam).
  • Natural Gas: Found with or without petroleum. Considered an environment-friendly fuel due to low carbon dioxide emissions. The HVJ (Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur) pipeline is a major artery.
  • Electricity: Generated mainly in two ways: Hydroelectricity (running water driving turbines, renewable) and Thermal electricity (burning fossil fuels like coal, petroleum, non-renewable).

B. Non-Conventional Sources of Energy

Growing consumption of fossil fuels has led to environmental problems and the fear of depletion. Thus, there is a pressing need for renewable energy sources.

  • Solar Energy: India is a tropical country. Photovoltaic technology converts sunlight directly into electricity. It is becoming extremely popular in rural and remote areas.
  • Wind Power: India has great potential for wind power. The largest wind farm cluster is in Tamil Nadu (Nagercoil to Madurai).
  • Biogas: Shrubs, farm waste, animal, and human waste are used to produce biogas. It yields higher thermal efficiency than kerosene or dung cake. It also provides excellent manure.
  • Tidal Energy: Oceanic tides can be used to generate electricity by building dams across narrow creek openings. (Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kachchh).
  • Geo-Thermal Energy: Heat and electricity produced by using the heat from the interior of the Earth. Where the geothermal gradient is high, groundwater gets heated, rises as steam, and drives turbines. (Parvati Valley, Puga Valley).

5. Conservation of Energy Resources

Every sector of the national economy needs energy. "Energy saved is energy produced." Promotion of energy conservation and increased use of renewable energy sources are the twin planks of sustainable energy. We must use public transport, switch off electrical devices when not in use, and use power-saving devices.
