Lifelines of National Economy

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 7 • Detailed Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Importance of Transport and Communication

The pace of development of a country depends upon the production of goods and services as well as their movement over space. Therefore, efficient means of transport and communication are prerequisites for fast development. Transport, communication, and trade are complementary to each other.

2. Roadways

India has one of the largest road networks in the world. Roadways have an edge over railways because:

  • Construction cost of roads is much lower than railway lines.
  • Roads can traverse comparatively more dissected and undulating topography (hills and mountains).
  • It provides door-to-door service, reducing the cost of loading and unloading.
  • Road transport is also used as a feeder to other modes of transport (e.g., provides a link between railway stations, airports, and seaports).

Classification of Roads in India

  • Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways: A major project linking Delhi-Kolkata-Chennai-Mumbai-Delhi by six-lane Super Highways to reduce time and distance between mega cities. Maintained by NHAI.
  • National Highways: Link extreme parts of the country. Maintained by CPWD. (e.g., NH-1 is Sher-Shah Suri Marg between Delhi and Amritsar).
  • State Highways: Link state capitals with district headquarters. Maintained by State Public Works Department (PWD).
  • District Roads: Connect the district headquarters with other places in the district. Maintained by Zila Parishad.
  • Rural Roads: Receive special impetus under the Pradhan Mantri Grameen Sadak Yojana (providing all-season motorable roads to every village).
  • Border Roads: Border Roads Organisation (BRO), established in 1960, constructs and maintains roads in the bordering areas of the country. Vital for strategic and economic development of northern/northeastern borders.

Road Density: The length of road per 100 sq. km of area. Density varies widely from dense in Kerala to sparse in Jammu & Kashmir.

3. Railways

Railways are the principal mode of transportation for freight and passengers in India, making possible multi-farious activities like business, sightseeing, pilgrimage, and transportation of bulky goods over long distances.

  • Distribution Challenges: The Northern Plains are ideal due to high population and flat land, but multiple river crossings require expensive bridges. Hilly terrains (Himalayas, Peninsular hills) make laying tracks difficult and costly. Sandy plains of Rajasthan, swamps of Gujarat, and forested tracks of MP/Jharkhand also present huge hurdles.
  • Gauge Types: Broad Gauge (1.676m), Meter Gauge (1.000m), and Narrow Gauge (0.762m/0.610m).

4. Pipelines

A relatively new arrival on the transportation map of India. Initially used only for transporting water to cities/industries. Now used for transporting crude oil, petroleum products, and natural gas from oil/natural gas fields to refineries, fertilizer factories, and big thermal power plants.

Advantages: Initial cost of laying pipelines is high, but subsequent running costs are minimal. It rules out trans-shipment losses or delays. Slurry can be transported if solids are processed into it.

5. Waterways

The cheapest means of transport. Most suitable for carrying heavy and bulky goods. It is a fuel-efficient and environment-friendly mode of transport.

  • National Waterway 1 (NW-1): Ganga river between Allahabad and Haldia.
  • National Waterway 2 (NW-2): Brahmaputra river between Sadiya and Dhubri.
  • National Waterway 3 (NW-3): West Coast Canal in Kerala.

Major Sea Ports

India's long coastline is dotted with 12 major and over 200 notified non-major ports. They handle 95% of India's foreign trade.

West Coast: Kandla (Deendayal Port - tidal port developed to ease Mumbai port), Mumbai (biggest port with a spacious natural harbor), Marmagao (premier iron ore export port), New Mangalore, Kochi.
East Coast: Tuticorin (natural harbor), Chennai (one of the oldest artificial ports), Visakhapatnam (deepest landlocked and well-protected port), Paradip (iron ore), Haldia/Kolkata.

6. Airways

The fastest, most comfortable, and prestigious mode of transport. It can easily cover very difficult terrains like high mountains, dreary deserts, and dense forests.

Air India provides international and domestic air services. Pawan Hans Helicopters Ltd. provides helicopter services to ONGC in its off-shore operations and to inaccessible hilly areas.

7. Communication and Trade

  • Mass Communication: Provides entertainment and creates awareness among people about various national programs and policies. Includes radio, television, newspapers, magazines, books, and films. India publishes a large number of newspapers in over 100 languages.
  • International Trade: The exchange of goods among people, states, and countries is trade. Trade between two countries is international trade.
    Balance of Trade: The difference between export and import. A favorable balance of trade occurs when the value of exports exceeds imports.
  • Tourism as a Trade: Promotes national integration, provides support to local handicrafts, and helps develop an international understanding of our culture and heritage. It brings in massive foreign exchange.
