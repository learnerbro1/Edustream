Manufacturing Industries

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 6 • Detailed Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Importance of Manufacturing

Production of goods in large quantities after processing from raw materials to more valuable products is called manufacturing. It belongs to the Secondary Sector. The economic strength of a country is measured by the development of its manufacturing industries.

  • Modernizes Agriculture: It helps in modernizing agriculture, which forms the backbone of our economy. It reduces people's heavy dependence on agricultural income by providing jobs in secondary and tertiary sectors.
  • Eradicates Poverty: Industrial development is a precondition for the eradication of unemployment and poverty.
  • Export of Manufactured Goods: Expands trade and commerce, bringing in much-needed foreign exchange.
  • Increases Wealth: Countries that transform their raw materials into a wide variety of furnished goods of higher value are prosperous.
Agriculture and Industry go hand-in-hand: They are not exclusive of each other. Agro-industries have given a major boost to agriculture by raising its productivity (e.g., providing fertilizers, tractors, PVC pipes, while using agricultural raw materials like cotton and sugarcane).

2. Industrial Location

Industrial locations are complex in nature. They are influenced by the availability of:

  • Raw materials (needs to be cheap and abundant)
  • Labor (skilled and unskilled)
  • Capital (finance and banking)
  • Power/Energy
  • Market (to sell finished products)

It is rarely possible to find all these factors available at one place. Consequently, manufacturing activity tends to locate at the most appropriate place where all factors are either available or can be arranged at lower cost. This is known as Agglomeration Economics (industries coming together to use city facilities).

3. Classification of Industries

A. On the basis of Raw Materials used:

Agro-Based: Use agricultural raw materials. (Cotton, woollen, jute, silk textile, rubber, sugar, tea, coffee, edible oil).
Mineral-Based: Use minerals and metals as raw materials. (Iron and steel, cement, aluminum, machine tools, petrochemicals).

B. On the basis of their Main Role:

  • Basic or Key Industries: Supply their products or raw materials to manufacture other goods (e.g., Iron and steel, copper smelting, aluminum smelting).
  • Consumer Industries: Produce goods for direct use by consumers (e.g., Sugar, toothpaste, paper, sewing machines, fans).

C. On the basis of Capital Investment:

Classified into Small-scale (investment up to a certain limit, usually Rs 1 crore) and Large-scale industries.

D. On the basis of Ownership:

  • Public Sector: Owned and operated by government agencies (BHEL, SAIL).
  • Private Sector: Owned and operated by individuals or a group of individuals (TISCO, Bajaj Auto, Reliance).
  • Joint Sector: Jointly run by the state and individuals (Oil India Ltd).
  • Cooperative Sector: Owned and operated by the producers/suppliers of raw materials, workers, or both. They pool resources and share profits/losses (Sugar industry in Maharashtra, Coir industry in Kerala, AMUL).

4. Major Agro-Based Industries

  • Textile Industry: The only industry in the country which is self-reliant and complete in the value chain (from raw material to highest value-added products). It contributes significantly to industrial production, employment, and foreign exchange.
    - Cotton Textiles: Historically hand-spinning. Now concentrated in Maharashtra and Gujarat due to availability of raw cotton, market, transport, and moist climate.
    - Jute Textiles: India is the largest producer of raw jute. Most mills are located in West Bengal along the banks of the Hugli river. (Factors: Cheap water transport, abundant water for processing, cheap labor from adjoining states).
  • Sugar Industry: India stands second as a world producer of sugar but first in gur and khandsari. The industry is seasonal, hence suited to the cooperative sector. There is a recent tendency for mills to shift to the South and West (especially Maharashtra) because the cane produced there has a higher sucrose content.

5. Major Mineral-Based Industries

  • Iron and Steel Industry: The basic industry. Often called a heavy industry because all raw materials (iron ore, coking coal, limestone) and finished products are heavy and bulky. Chotanagpur plateau region has the maximum concentration of iron and steel industries (low cost of iron ore, high-grade raw materials, cheap labor).
  • Aluminum Smelting: Second most important metallurgical industry. It is light, rust-resistant, good conductor of heat, malleable, and becomes strong when mixed with other metals. Used in aircraft, utensils, and wires. Bauxite is the raw material.
  • Chemical, Fertilizer, and Cement Industries: Extremely crucial for infrastructure development and agricultural growth. Cement requires bulky raw materials like limestone, silica, and gypsum.
  • Information Technology and Electronics: Covers a wide range of products from transistor sets to television, telephones, cellular telecom, pagers, radars, and computers. Bengaluru is known as the electronic capital of India.

6. Industrial Pollution and Environmental Degradation

Industries are responsible for four types of pollution:

  • Air Pollution: Caused by high proportions of undesirable gases (sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide). Airborne particulate materials (dust, sprays, mist, smoke from chemical/paper factories, brick kilns, refineries).
  • Water Pollution: Caused by organic and inorganic industrial wastes and effluents discharged into rivers. Major culprits are paper, pulp, chemical, textile, and dyeing industries (fly ash, phospo-gypsum).
  • Thermal Pollution: Occurs when hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers and ponds before cooling, causing massive damage to aquatic life.
  • Noise Pollution: Causes irritation, anger, hearing impairment, increased heart rate, and blood pressure. (Generators, compressors, industrial drills).
Control of Environmental Degradation:
  • Minimizing use of water by reusing and recycling it in two or more successive stages.
  • Harvesting rainwater to meet water requirements.
  • Treating hot water and effluents before releasing them (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary treatment).
  • Fitting smokestacks with electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers, and inertial separators to reduce particulate matter in the air.
