The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

CBSE Class 10 • History — Chapter 1 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Frédéric Sorrieu's Vision

In 1848, a French artist prepared four prints visualizing his dream of a world made up of 'democratic and social Republics'. It showed people of Europe and America marching towards the Statue of Liberty, offering homage. It was an Utopian vision where the absolute institutions (Absolutist) lay shattered on the ground.

Fig 1.1: Frédéric Sorrieu's Utopian Vision (1848)

2. The French Revolution & Idea of the Nation

The first clear expression of nationalism came with the French Revolution in 1789.

  • Introduction of ideas like la patrie (the fatherland) and le citoyen (the citizen).
  • A new French flag (the tricolour) replaced the former royal standard.
  • Uniform laws, a centralized administrative system, and uniform weights & measures were adopted.
The Napoleonic Code (Civil Code of 1804):
  • Did away with all privileges based on birth.
  • Established equality before the law and secured the right to property.
  • Simplified administrative divisions and abolished the feudal system.

3. The Making of Nationalism in Europe

Europe in the mid-18th century had no 'nation-states'. It was divided into kingdoms, duchies, and cantons whose rulers had autonomous territories.

The Aristocracy: Dominant class, owned estates, spoke French for diplomacy, bound by ties of marriage.
The New Middle Class: Emerged due to industrialization. Composed of industrialists, businessmen, and professionals who demanded liberal nationalism.

4. Unification Movements

Unification of Germany (1866-1871)

Led by Prussia under Chief Minister Otto von Bismarck. Using the Prussian army and bureaucracy, he fought 3 wars over 7 years (with Austria, Denmark, and France). Prussia won, and King William I was proclaimed German Emperor in 1871.

Unification of Italy

Italy was divided into 7 states. Only Sardinia-Piedmont was ruled by an Italian princely house.

  • Giuseppe Mazzini: Formed secret societies like Young Italy.
  • Count Cavour: Led the movement to unify Italy through diplomatic alliances.
  • Giuseppe Garibaldi: Led armed volunteers (Red Shirts) to drive out the Spanish from the South.

In 1861, Victor Emmanuel II was proclaimed king of united Italy.

5. Visualising the Nation

Nations were portrayed as female figures (Allegories).

  • Marianne: Represented the Republic of France. Wore the red cap, the tricolour, and the cockade.
  • Germania: Allegory of the German nation. Wears a crown of oak leaves, as the German oak stands for heroism.
