Forest & Wildlife Resources

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 2 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Biodiversity / Biological Diversity

The immense variety of life on Earth. India is one of the world's richest countries in terms of its vast array of biological diversity (nearly 8% of the total number of species in the world).

2. IUCN Classification of Species

Based on the International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources (IUCN):

  • Normal Species: Population levels are considered normal for their survival (e.g., cattle, sal, pine).
  • Endangered Species: In danger of extinction if negative factors continue to operate (e.g., Black buck, crocodile, Indian rhino, sangai).
  • Vulnerable Species: Population has declined to levels where it is likely to move into the endangered category soon (e.g., Blue sheep, Asiatic elephant).
  • Rare Species: Small populations that may move into endangered/vulnerable category if not protected (e.g., Hornbill, Desert fox).
  • Endemic Species: Found only in some particular areas usually isolated by natural barriers (e.g., Andaman teal, Nicobar pigeon).
  • Extinct Species: Not found after searches of known or likely areas (e.g., Asiatic cheetah, Pink head duck).

3. Causes of Depletion

Colonial Policies: Large-scale commercial forestry, expansion of railways, and agriculture during British rule ruined vast tracts of Indian forests.
Modern Activities: Agricultural expansion, large river valley projects (Narmada Sagar), mining (Buxa Tiger Reserve), and over-grazing.

4. Conservation of Forest and Wildlife

Conservation preserves the ecological diversity and our life support systems (water, air, and soil).

The Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972:

Implemented to protect habitats. It published an all-India list of protected species, banned hunting, and restricted trade in wildlife. Many National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries were established.

Project Tiger (1973)

A globally significant wildlife campaign initiated to save the endangered tiger population in India. Famous reserves include Corbett (Uttarakhand), Sundarbans (West Bengal), Bandhavgarh (MP), and Sariska (Rajasthan).

5. Types and Distribution of Forests

  • Reserved Forests: Regarded as the most valuable for conservation (more than half of total forest land).
  • Protected Forests: Protected from any further depletion (almost one-third of total).
  • Unclassed Forests: Forests belonging to both govt and private individuals.

6. Community and Conservation

Local communities often struggle to conserve habitats.

  • Bishnoi community in Rajasthan actively protects blackbucks.
  • Chipko Movement in the Himalayas successfully resisted deforestation by hugging trees.
  • Joint Forest Management (JFM): Involves local communities in the management and restoration of degraded forests. Odisha passed the first JFM resolution in 1988.
