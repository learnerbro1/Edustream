Sectors of the Indian Economy

CBSE Class 10 • Economics — Chapter 2 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Three Sectors of Economic Activities

  • Primary Sector (Agriculture & Related): When we produce a good by exploiting natural resources. E.g., Agriculture, dairy, fishing, forestry, mining.
  • Secondary Sector (Industrial): Covers activities in which natural products are changed into other forms through ways of manufacturing. E.g., using cotton fiber to spin yarn, making sugar from sugarcane.
  • Tertiary Sector (Service): Activities that help in the development of the primary and secondary sectors. These activities, by themselves, do not produce a good but they are an aid for production. E.g., Transport, storage, communication, banking, teachers, doctors.

2. Comparing the Three Sectors (GDP)

To count the various goods and services, we use the value of final goods and services.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): It is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year. It shows how big the economy is.

Why only Final Goods? To avoid the problem of double counting. Intermediate goods are already included in the value of the final good (e.g., value of flour is already included in the value of biscuits).

3. Historical Change and Rising Importance of Tertiary Sector

Over the forty years between 1973-74 and 2013-14, production in all three sectors has increased, but it has increased the most in the tertiary sector.

  • Basic services like hospitals, educational institutions, post, police are required in any developing country.
  • Development of agriculture and industry leads to the development of services such as transport, trade, storage.
  • As income levels rise, people demand more services like eating out, tourism, shopping, private hospitals.
  • Information and communication technology services have become essential.

4. Underemployment / Disguised Unemployment

While the tertiary sector's share in GDP has grown immensely, employment hasn't shifted as much. More than half of the workers in the country are still working in the primary sector, producing only a quarter of the GDP.

Disguised Unemployment: A situation where people are apparently working, but all of them are made to work less than their potential. Even if a few people are removed, production will not be affected. Also called underemployment.

How to create more employment? Provide cheap loans, invest in dams/canals, improve rural transportation, promote local industries in semi-rural areas, and improve health/education sectors. (MGNREGA 2005 guarantees 100 days of employment in a year).

5. Organised vs Unorganised Sector

Organised Sector: Terms of employment are regular. Registered by the govt, follows rules and regulations (Factories Act, Minimum Wages Act). Workers enjoy security of employment, paid leaves, provident fund.
Unorganised Sector: Small and scattered units outside the control of the govt. Jobs are low-paid and often not regular. No provision for overtime, paid leave, holidays, or sickness leave.

6. Public vs Private Sector

  • Public Sector: The government owns most of the assets and provides all the services (e.g., Railways, Post Office). Purpose is not just to earn profits but social welfare.
  • Private Sector: Ownership of assets and delivery of services is in the hands of private individuals or companies (e.g., TISCO, Reliance). Driven mainly by the motive to earn profits.
