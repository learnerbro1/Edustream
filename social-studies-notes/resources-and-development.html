Resources and Development

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 1 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. What is a Resource?

Everything available in our environment which can be used to satisfy our needs, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible, and culturally acceptable.

Classification of Resources

  • On the basis of Origin: Biotic (living, e.g., flora/fauna) and Abiotic (non-living, e.g., rocks/metals).
  • On the basis of Exhaustibility: Renewable (solar, wind) and Non-renewable (fossil fuels).
  • On the basis of Ownership: Individual, Community-owned, National (within political boundaries and 12 nautical miles of ocean), and International.
  • On the basis of Status of Development: Potential (found but not utilized), Developed (surveyed for quality/quantity), Stock (no technology to extract), and Reserve (can be used but kept for future).

2. Sustainable Development & Agenda 21

Development should take place without damaging the environment, and it shouldn't compromise the needs of future generations.

Earth Summit & Agenda 21 (1992):

Held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. It was a global declaration to combat environmental damage, poverty, and disease through global co-operation. Every local government was asked to draw its own local Agenda 21.

3. Land Resources and Degradation

India's land features: Plains (43%), Mountains (30%), Plateaus (27%).

Causes of Degradation: Over-grazing (Gujarat, Rajasthan), Over-irrigation causing waterlogging and salinity (Punjab, Haryana), Mining (Jharkhand, Odisha).
Conservation Methods: Afforestation, proper management of grazing, control on mining activities, drip irrigation, planting shelter belts.

4. Soil as a Resource

Soil is a living system and the most important renewable natural resource.

Fig 1.1: Distribution of Major Soil Types in India
  • Alluvial Soil: Most widely spread (Northern plains). Very fertile, contains potash, phosphoric acid, and lime. Ideal for wheat, paddy, sugarcane.
  • Black Soil (Regur): Black in color, ideal for growing cotton. Found in Deccan trap (Maharashtra, Saurashtra). High moisture retention capacity.
  • Red and Yellow Soil: Red due to iron diffusion. Yellow when hydrated. Found in parts of Odisha, Chhattisgarh.
  • Laterite Soil: Forms under high temperature and heavy rainfall (leaching). Good for tea, coffee, cashew nuts (with fertilizer).
  • Arid Soil: Sandy and saline. Lacks humus and moisture.

5. Soil Erosion and Conservation

The denudation of the soil cover and subsequent washing down is described as soil erosion.

Measures: Contour ploughing, terrace farming (on hills), and strip cropping (planting strips of grass between crops to break wind force).
