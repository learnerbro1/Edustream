Power Sharing

CBSE Class 10 • Political Science — Chapter 1 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Story of Belgium

Belgium is a small country in Europe. It has a complex ethnic composition:

  • Country level: 59% speak Dutch (Flemish region), 40% speak French (Wallonia region), 1% speak German.
  • Capital (Brussels) level: 80% speak French, 20% speak Dutch.

The Conflict: The minority French-speaking community was relatively rich and powerful. This created tensions with the Dutch-speaking community during the 1950s and 1960s.

Accommodation in Belgium: Between 1970 and 1993, the constitution was amended 4 times. Key elements: Equal number of Dutch and French ministers in the central govt, more powers to state govts, a separate govt in Brussels with equal representation, and a third kind of govt called Community Government (elected by people belonging to one language community).

2. Story of Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka emerged as an independent country in 1948. Population composition: 74% Sinhala speakers (mostly Buddhist) and 18% Tamil speakers (Hindu/Muslim).

Majoritarianism: A belief that the majority community should be able to rule a country in whichever way it wants, by disregarding the wishes of the minority.
  • In 1956, an Act was passed to recognize Sinhala as the only official language.
  • Govt followed preferential policies favoring Sinhala applicants for university and govt jobs.
  • A new constitution stipulated that the state shall protect and foster Buddhism.

Impact: Increased feeling of alienation among Sri Lankan Tamils. They demanded equal rights and regional autonomy. Refusal led to a Civil War, causing terrible social, cultural, and economic life setbacks.

3. Why Power Sharing is Desirable?

Prudential Reason: Based on careful calculation of gains and losses. Power sharing helps to reduce the possibility of conflict between social groups, ensuring political stability.
Moral Reason: Based on moral considerations. Power sharing is the very spirit of democracy. People have a right to be consulted on how they are to be governed.

4. Forms of Power Sharing

  • Horizontal distribution: Shared among different organs of government (Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary). Ensures a system of checks and balances.
  • Vertical distribution: Shared among governments at different levels (Federal government, State government, Municipality/Panchayat).
  • Among different social groups: Religious and linguistic groups (e.g., Community Government in Belgium).
  • Political parties, pressure groups, and movements: In democracies, power is shared among different political parties representing different ideologies (e.g., Coalition Governments).
