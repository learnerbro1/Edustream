Agriculture

CBSE Class 10 • Geography — Chapter 4 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Types of Farming

  • Primitive Subsistence Farming: Practiced on small patches of land using primitive tools (hoe, digging sticks) and family labor. Depends on monsoons and natural fertility. Also known as 'Slash and burn' agriculture (called Jhumming in NE India).
  • Intensive Subsistence Farming: Practiced in areas of high population pressure. It is labor-intensive, using high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation to get higher yields.
  • Commercial Farming: Use of higher doses of modern inputs (HYV seeds, fertilizers, pesticides) to obtain higher productivity for the market. Plantation is a type of commercial farming where a single crop is grown on a large area (e.g., Tea in Assam, Coffee in Karnataka).

3. Major Crops

Rice (Kharif): Staple food. Needs high temperature (>25°C) and high humidity with annual rainfall >100cm. Major areas: Northern plains, coastal areas.
Wheat (Rabi): Second most important cereal. Needs a cool growing season and bright sunshine at ripening. Rainfall: 50-75cm. Major areas: Punjab, Haryana.
  • Millets: Jowar, Bajra, Ragi. High nutritional value. Ragi is rich in iron and calcium.
  • Sugarcane: Tropical/subtropical crop. Needs hot and humid climate (21°-27°C) and 75-100cm rainfall. India is the 2nd largest producer after Brazil.
  • Tea: Beverage crop. Needs warm, moist, frost-free climate with frequent showers. Labor intensive. (Assam, Darjeeling).

4. Technological and Institutional Reforms

To improve agriculture, the government introduced several reforms:

  • Institutional: Abolition of Zamindari, consolidation of land holdings, establishment of Grameen banks, cooperative societies for loans.
  • Technological: Green Revolution (use of HYV seeds), White Revolution (Operation Flood for milk), issuing Kissan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS).

Bhoodan-Gramdan: Initiated by Vinoba Bhave. It was a voluntary land reform movement (Bloodless Revolution) where rich landlords donated land to the landless.
