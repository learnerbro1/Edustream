Globalisation & the Indian Economy

CBSE Class 10 • Economics — Chapter 4 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Multinational Corporations (MNCs)

An MNC is a company that owns or controls production in more than one nation. They set up offices and factories for production in regions where they can get cheap labour and other resources.

  • Foreign Investment: Investment made by MNCs to buy assets such as land, building, machines, etc.
  • Ways MNCs spread their production: 1. Setting up partnerships with local companies. 2. Buying local companies. 3. Placing orders with small producers (e.g., garments, footwear, sports items).

Result: Production in widely dispersed locations is getting interlinked.

2. What is Globalisation?

Globalisation: It is the process of rapid integration or interconnection between countries. More and more goods and services, investments and technology are moving between countries.

Factors that have enabled Globalisation:

  • Technology: Rapid improvement in technology (transportation, container services) and Information & Communication Technology (Internet, mobile phones).
  • Liberalisation: Removing barriers or restrictions set by the government on foreign trade and foreign investment.

3. Trade Barriers and Liberalisation in India

Trade Barrier: Some restrictions imposed by the government on foreign trade (e.g., tax on imports). It is used to increase/decrease foreign trade and to protect domestic producers.

India's case: After Independence, India put barriers to protect producers from foreign competition. But around 1991, a major shift in policy was made. The government decided that the time had come for Indian producers to compete with producers around the globe to improve quality. Barriers on foreign trade and investment were removed to a large extent (Liberalisation).

4. World Trade Organisation (WTO)

An international organisation whose aim is to liberalise international trade. Started at the initiative of the developed countries, WTO establishes rules regarding international trade for all countries. However, developing countries often complain that WTO rules are biased and force them to remove trade barriers while developed countries illegally retain them.

5. Impact of Globalisation in India

Positive Impact: Greater choice and lower prices for consumers. Higher standard of living. Indian companies like Tata Motors, Infosys, Ranbaxy emerged as MNCs. New jobs created in IT sector.
Negative Impact: Small producers (batteries, toys, tires) have been hit hard due to competition and many shut down. Workers' jobs are no longer secure; they are hired 'flexibly' to cut costs.

6. Struggle for a Fair Globalisation

Fair globalisation would create opportunities for all and ensure that the benefits are shared better. The government can play a major role by ensuring labor laws are properly implemented, protecting small producers, and negotiating at the WTO with other developing countries for fairer rules.
