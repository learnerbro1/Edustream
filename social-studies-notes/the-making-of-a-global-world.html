The Making of a Global World

CBSE Class 10 • History — Chapter 3 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. The Pre-Modern World

Globalization isn't a new phenomenon. Travelers, traders, priests, and pilgrims traveled vast distances for knowledge, opportunity, and spiritual fulfillment.

  • Silk Routes: Vibrant pre-modern trade and cultural links between distant parts of the world. Used to transport Chinese silk, Indian spices, and exchange Buddhism/Christianity.
  • Food Travels: Foods like potatoes, soya, groundnuts, maize, tomatoes, and chillies were introduced to Europe and Asia after Christopher Columbus accidentally discovered the Americas.

Irish Potato Famine: Europe's poor became so dependent on potatoes that when a disease destroyed the potato crop in Ireland (1840s), hundreds of thousands died of starvation.

2. The Nineteenth Century (1815-1914)

Economists identify three types of flows within international economic exchanges:

1. Flow of Trade: Trade in goods (e.g., cloth or wheat).
2. Flow of Labor: Migration of people searching for work.
3. Flow of Capital: Short-term or long-term investments over long distances.

Role of Technology

Railways, steamships, and the telegraph transformed the 19th-century world.
Meat Trade: Refrigerated ships allowed perishable foods like meat to be transported from America/Australia to Europe, lowering meat prices and improving European diets.

3. Imperialism and Colonialism

  • Rinderpest (Cattle Plague): In the 1890s, a fast-spreading cattle plague killed 90% of cattle in Africa. This destroyed African livelihoods, forcing them into the labor market to work in European mines and plantations.
  • Indentured Labour from India: Hundreds of thousands of Indians/Chinese were hired under contracts (indenture) to work on plantations and mines in the Caribbean, Fiji, and Mauritius. It was described as a new system of slavery.

4. The Inter-War Economy & The Great Depression

The First World War transformed the US from an international debtor to an international creditor.

The Great Depression (1929 onwards):

Caused by overproduction in agriculture, collapse of US banks, and withdrawal of US loans. It led to massive unemployment, bank failures, and ruined economies globally. India's agricultural prices fell drastically, heavily impacting peasants.

5. Rebuilding a World Economy (Bretton Woods)

After WW2, the Bretton Woods Conference (1944) established:

  • IMF (International Monetary Fund): To deal with external surpluses and deficits.
  • World Bank: To finance post-war reconstruction.

G-77: Developing nations organized themselves into the Group of 77 to demand a New International Economic Order (NIEO) for fairer trade and control over their natural resources.
