Magnetic Effects of Current

CBSE Class 10 • Physics — Chapter 12 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Magnetic Field and Field Lines

The region around a magnet where its force can be detected. Magnetic field lines indicate the direction and strength of the field.

Fig 12.1: Magnetic field lines around a bar magnet
  • They emerge from North pole and merge at South pole (outside the magnet). Inside, they go South to North.
  • They form closed, continuous curves.
  • Two magnetic field lines never intersect. (If they did, the compass would point in two directions at once, which is impossible).
  • Closer lines = Stronger magnetic field.

2. Magnetic Field Due to Current-Carrying Conductor

Straight Conductor: Forms concentric circles. Direction found by Right-Hand Thumb Rule: If you point your right thumb in the direction of current, your curled fingers show the direction of magnetic field.

Circular Loop: Field is straight and parallel at the center of the loop.

Fig 12.2: Magnetic field due to current in a Solenoid

Solenoid: A coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire. Its magnetic field is exactly like a bar magnet. A strong magnetic field inside a solenoid can magnetize soft iron placed inside it (forming an Electromagnet).

3. Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force (maximum when placed perpendicular to the field).

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:
Stretch thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular.
  • Forefinger → Magnetic Field (B)
  • Centre finger → Current (I)
  • Thumb → Motion / Force / Thrust (F)

Application: Electric Motors, Loudspeakers.

4. Domestic Electric Circuits

  • Live wire: Red insulation (+ 220V)
  • Neutral wire: Black insulation (0V)
  • Earth wire: Green insulation. Connected to a metal plate deep in the earth. Provides a low-resistance path to protect users from severe electric shocks due to leakage.
Short Circuit: When live and neutral wires come in direct contact, resistance drops to almost zero, and current shoots up dangerously.
Overloading: Connecting too many heavy appliances to a single socket, causing overheating.

Electric Fuse: A safety device working on Joule's heating. It melts and breaks the circuit during overloading/short-circuiting.
