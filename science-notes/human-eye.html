Human Eye & Colourful World

CBSE Class 10 • Physics — Chapter 10 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. The Human Eye

Acts like a camera, focusing light onto a light-sensitive screen called the retina.

Fig 10.1: Schematic diagram of the Human Eye
  • Cornea: Front transparent bulge where maximum refraction occurs.
  • Iris & Pupil: Iris controls the size of the pupil, regulating the amount of light entering.
  • Eye Lens: Crystalline convex lens that forms a real and inverted image on the retina.
  • Ciliary Muscles: Modify the curvature (and focal length) of the eye lens.
  • Retina: Contains photoreceptors (rods for dim light, cones for color).

Power of Accommodation: The ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length. Normal near point = 25 cm. Far point = Infinity.

2. Defects of Vision

Myopia (Near-sightedness): Can see near objects clearly. Image forms in front of the retina.
Cause: Elongation of eyeball or excessive curvature of lens.
Correction: Concave lens.
Hypermetropia (Far-sightedness): Can see far objects clearly. Image forms behind the retina.
Cause: Eyeball too small or focal length too long.
Correction: Convex lens.
Fig 10.2: Defect of Vision and their corrections using lenses

Presbyopia: Age-related loss of accommodation (weakening of ciliary muscles). Corrected using Bifocal lenses.

3. Refraction and Dispersion via Prism

  • Refraction through a Prism: The emergent ray bends towards the base of the prism. The angle between incident ray and emergent ray is the Angle of Deviation.
  • Dispersion: Splitting of white light into 7 colors (VIBGYOR). Red bends the least, Violet bends the most.
  • Rainbow Formation: Caused by dispersion, internal reflection, and refraction of sunlight by tiny water droplets.
Fig 10.3: Dispersion of white light by a glass prism

4. Atmospheric Refraction & Scattering

Refraction of light by earth's atmosphere due to changing optical densities.

  • Twinkling of stars: Continuous changing of atmospheric refraction causes starlight to fluctuate. (Planets don't twinkle as they are closer extended sources).
  • Advance sunrise and delayed sunset: Sun is visible 2 mins before actual sunrise due to bending of light.
  • Scattering (Tyndall Effect): Fine particles scatter shorter wavelengths (blue) strongly. This causes the blue colour of sky and the red colour of sun at sunrise/sunset.
