Carbon and its Compounds

CBSE Class 10 • Chemistry — Chapter 4 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Bonding in Carbon

Carbon (Atomic No. 6, Configuration 2,4) forms Covalent Bonds by sharing electrons. It cannot easily lose 4 electrons (requires huge energy) or gain 4 electrons (nucleus can't hold 10 electrons).

Fig 4.1: Electron Dot Structure of Methane (CH₄) showing shared electrons
Versatile Nature of Carbon:
  • Catenation: Ability to form long chains, branches, and rings with other carbon atoms.
  • Tetravalency: Having a valency of 4, it can bond with 4 other atoms of carbon or some other monovalent element.

2. Hydrocarbons

Saturated (Alkanes): Single bonds between C-C atoms.
Formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
E.g., Methane (CH₄), Ethane (C₂H₆).
Unsaturated: Double or triple bonds between C-C atoms.
Alkenes: CₙH₂ₙ (Double bond)
Alkynes: CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (Triple bond).

3. Functional Groups

Group NameFormula/StructureSuffix
Alcohol-OH-ol (e.g., Ethanol)
Aldehyde-CHO-al (e.g., Ethanal)
Ketone-C=O-one (e.g., Propanone)
Carboxylic Acid-COOH-oic acid (e.g., Ethanoic acid)

4. Chemical Properties

  • Combustion: Carbon compounds burn in O₂ to give CO₂, H₂O, heat, and light. Saturated give clean flame; unsaturated give yellow, sooty flame.
  • Addition Reaction: Hydrogen is added to unsaturated hydrocarbons in the presence of catalysts (Palladium/Nickel) to give saturated hydrocarbons. (Used in hydrogenation of vegetable oils).
  • Substitution Reaction: In presence of sunlight, chlorine can replace hydrogen atoms in alkanes (e.g., CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl).

5. Important Compounds & Soap

  • Ethanol (C₂H₅OH): Active ingredient in alcoholic drinks. Good solvent.
    Reaction with Na: Forms Sodium ethoxide and H₂ gas.
  • Ethanoic Acid (CH₃COOH): 5-8% solution in water is vinegar.
    Esterification: Reacts with ethanol to form sweet-smelling Esters.
  • Soaps and Detergents: Soap molecules have a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and a hydrophobic (water-fearing) tail. They form micelles in water to trap dirt.

★ Quick memory tips

Homologous Series: A series of compounds with the same functional group. Successive members differ by a -CH₂ unit and 14u mass. They have similar chemical properties but differing physical properties.
