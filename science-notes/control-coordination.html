Control and Coordination

CBSE Class 10 • Biology — Chapter 6 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Nervous System in Humans

Provides fast, short-lived responses using electrical impulses. The functional unit is the Neuron (Nerve cell).

Fig 6.1: Structure of a Neuron (Nerve cell)
  • Synapse: The microscopic gap between a pair of adjacent neurons over which nerve impulses pass via neurotransmitters.
  • Reflex Action: Sudden, involuntary response to a stimulus. The pathway taken by the nerve impulse is the Reflex Arc (Receptor → Sensory Neuron → Spinal Cord → Motor Neuron → Effector).

2. Human Brain

Fore-brain: Cerebrum (thinking, memory, sensory processing). Main thinking part.
Hind-brain: Cerebellum (posture, balance, precision), Medulla (involuntary actions like blood pressure, vomiting).
Fig 6.2: Schematic diagram of the Human Brain

3. Coordination in Plants

Plants do not have a nervous system. They respond to stimuli via chemical messengers (phytohormones) and movements.

  • Tropic Movements: Directional growth movements in response to stimuli.
    - Phototropism: Towards light.
    - Geotropism: Towards gravity.
    - Chemotropism: Towards chemicals (e.g., pollen tube towards ovule).
    - Hydrotropism: Towards water.
  • Nastic Movements: Non-directional (e.g., dropping of Touch-me-not plant leaves).

4. Plant Hormones

HormoneFunction
AuxinsPromote cell elongation, growth of shoot tip.
GibberellinsPromote stem growth, seed germination.
CytokininsPromote cell division (abundant in fruits/seeds).
Abscisic Acid (ABA)Inhibits growth, causes wilting of leaves (stress hormone).

5. Endocrine System in Humans

Uses chemical messengers (Hormones) secreted directly into the blood by ductless glands.

  • Thyroid: Thyroxine (regulates metabolism). Iodine is essential; deficiency causes Goitre.
  • Pancreas: Insulin (regulates blood sugar). Deficiency causes Diabetes.
  • Adrenal: Adrenaline (fight or flight response).
  • Pituitary: Growth hormone (master gland).

★ Quick memory tips

Nervous control is fast but localized. Hormonal control is slow but widespread and sustained.
