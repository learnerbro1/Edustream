Our Environment

CBSE Class 10 • Biology — Chapter 13 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Ecosystem and its Components

An ecosystem comprises all interacting organisms in an area together with the non-living (abiotic) constituents of the environment.

  • Biotic Components: Producers (plants, algae), Consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores), Decomposers (bacteria, fungi).
  • Abiotic Components: Light, temperature, soil, water, wind.

Role of Decomposers: They break down complex dead organic matter into simpler inorganic substances that go back into the soil, keeping nutrients recycling.

2. Food Chain and Food Web

Food Chain: A linear sequence showing who eats whom (e.g., Grass → Deer → Tiger). Each step is called a Trophic Level.

Food Web: Interconnected network of multiple food chains.

Fig 13.1: Pyramid of Trophic Levels showing energy flow
10% Law (by Raymond Lindeman):
Only 10% of the energy is transferred to the next trophic level.
The remaining 90% is used in life processes or lost as heat. Because of this, food chains usually only have 3 or 4 trophic levels.

3. Biological Magnification

The phenomenon where harmful, non-biodegradable chemicals (like pesticides/DDT) accumulate and increase in concentration at each successive trophic level.

Note: Human beings occupy the top level in most food chains, so they get the maximum concentration of these harmful chemicals.

4. Ozone Layer and its Depletion

Ozone (O₃) is a deadly poison at the ground level, but in the stratosphere, it forms a protective shield that absorbs harmful Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun.

Fig 13.2: Formation of Ozone and its depletion by CFCs
  • Formation: High energy UV splits O₂ → O + O. Then O + O₂ → O₃.
  • Depletion: Caused by synthetic chemicals like CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons) used in refrigerators and fire extinguishers.
  • Impact: Increased UV reaching the earth causes skin cancer, cataracts, and damage to crops.

5. Waste Management

Biodegradable Waste: Can be broken down by biological processes (microbes). Example: Paper, food waste, cotton.
Non-Biodegradable Waste: Cannot be broken down naturally. Accumulates and harms the environment. Example: Plastics, glass, synthetic fibers.
