Acids, Bases and Salts

CBSE Class 10 • Chemistry — Chapter 2 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Introduction to Acids and Bases

Acids: Sour to taste. Turn blue litmus red. Give H⁺ ions in aqueous solution.
Example: HCl, H₂SO₄, Citric acid.
Bases: Bitter to taste and soapy to touch. Turn red litmus blue. Give OH⁻ ions in solution.
Example: NaOH, KOH, Mg(OH)₂.

Indicators

  • Natural: Litmus (from Lichen), Turmeric, Red cabbage leaves.
  • Synthetic: Methyl orange (Red in acid, Yellow in base), Phenolphthalein (Colorless in acid, Pink in base).
  • Olfactory: Vanilla, Onion, Clove (smell changes in acidic/basic media).

2. Chemical Properties

Reaction with Metals: Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen gas (H₂)

Fig 2.1: Testing H₂ gas with a burning candle (Pop Sound Test)

Pop sound test: H₂ gas burns with a pop sound when a burning matchstick is brought near.

Reaction with Metal Carbonates: Acid + Metal Carbonate → Salt + CO₂ + H₂O
Limewater test: CO₂ turns limewater milky due to the formation of insoluble Calcium Carbonate.

Neutralisation Reaction:
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

3. pH Scale

A scale for measuring hydrogen ion concentration. (0 to 14)

Fig 2.2: The pH scale showing Acidic, Neutral, and Basic regions
  • pH < 7: Acidic (Lower the pH, stronger the acid)
  • pH = 7: Neutral (Pure water, normal salt)
  • pH > 7: Basic (Higher the pH, stronger the base)

Importance of pH: Tooth decay starts when mouth pH is below 5.5. Plants need specific soil pH to grow.

★ Quick memory tips

Water of Crystallization: Fixed number of water molecules in one formula unit of a salt. E.g., CuSO₄·5H₂O (Blue vitriol). When heated, it loses water and turns white.
