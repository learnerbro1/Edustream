Real Numbers

CBSE Class 10 • Maths — Chapter 1 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Chapter Formulas

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (HCF & LCM Relation):

For any two positive integers a and b:

HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

2. Important Concepts

  • Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.
  • Finding HCF and LCM by Prime Factorisation:
    • HCF: Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor in the numbers.
    • LCM: Product of the greatest power of each prime factor involved in the numbers.
  • Proof of Irrationality: Let p be a prime number. If p divides a², then p divides a, where a is a positive integer. We use this theorem and the method of contradiction to prove that numbers like √2, √3, √5 are irrational.

Note: The sum or difference of a rational and an irrational number is irrational, and the product or quotient of a non-zero rational and an irrational number is irrational.

3. PYQ with Solutions

Very short / MCQ — 1 mark

Q1. The HCF of 96 and 404 is 4. Find the LCM of 96 and 404. CBSE 2019 [1]
Solution: We know that HCF × LCM = Product of two numbers.
4 × LCM = 96 × 404
LCM = (96 × 404) / 4 = 96 × 101 = 9696.

Short answer — 2/3 marks

Q2. Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational, given that √5 is irrational. IMPORTANT [2]
Solution: Let us assume, to the contrary, that 3 + 2√5 is rational.
Then we can find coprime integers a and b (b ≠ 0) such that 3 + 2√5 = a/b.
⇒ 2√5 = (a/b) - 3 = (a - 3b)/b
⇒ √5 = (a - 3b)/2b.
Since a and b are integers, (a - 3b)/2b is rational, and so √5 is rational.
But this contradicts the fact that √5 is irrational.
This contradiction has arisen because of our incorrect assumption. So, 3 + 2√5 is irrational.
Q3. Given that HCF (306, 657) = 9, find LCM (306, 657). [2]
Solution: LCM × HCF = Product of the two numbers
LCM × 9 = 306 × 657
LCM = (306 × 657) / 9 = 34 × 657 = 22338.

Long answer / HOTS — 5 marks

Q4. Prove that √2 is an irrational number. IMPORTANT CBSE 2020 [5]
Solution: Let us assume, to the contrary, that √2 is rational.
Then, √2 = a/b, where a and b are coprime integers and b ≠ 0.
Squaring both sides, 2 = a² / b² ⇒ 2b² = a² ... (Equation 1)
This means that 2 divides a². Therefore, 2 divides a.
Let a = 2c for some integer c.
Substituting a = 2c in Equation 1:
2b² = (2c)² ⇒ 2b² = 4c² ⇒ b² = 2c²
This means that 2 divides b². Therefore, 2 divides b.
Since 2 divides both a and b, a and b have at least 2 as a common factor.
This contradicts the fact that a and b are coprime. The contradiction arises due to our incorrect assumption.
Hence, √2 is irrational.
