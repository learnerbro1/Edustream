Electricity

CBSE Class 10 • Physics — Chapter 11 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Basic Concepts

  • Electric Current (I): Rate of flow of electric charge. Formula: I = Q / t. Unit: Ampere (A). Measured by Ammeter (connected in series).
  • Potential Difference (V): Work done to move a unit charge from one point to another. Formula: V = W / Q. Unit: Volt (V). Measured by Voltmeter (connected in parallel).

2. Ohm's Law

The current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided temperature remains constant.

Fig 11.1: V-I Graph verifying Ohm's Law (Slope gives Resistance)
V = I × R
(R = Resistance. Unit of R is Ohm (Ω))

3. Resistance and Resistivity

Factors affecting resistance: Directly proportional to length (L), inversely proportional to area of cross-section (A), depends on material and temperature.

R = ρ (L / A)

Where ρ (Rho) is Resistivity. Unit: Ohm-meter (Ωm). Resistivity is a property of the material and does not change with length or thickness.

4. Resistors in Series and Parallel

In Series: Current is same across all resistors. Total potential difference is the sum.
R_eq = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
In Parallel: Potential difference is same across all. Total current splits.
1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
Fig 11.2: Arrangement of resistors in Series vs Parallel

Parallel circuits are used in homes because if one appliance fails, others continue working, and each gets the full voltage.

5. Heating Effect of Electric Current (Joule's Law)

The heat produced in a resistor is directly proportional to the square of current (I²), resistance (R), and time (t).

H = I²Rt

Applications: Electric iron, toaster, heater, electric fuse, bulb filament (Tungsten).

6. Electric Power

Rate at which electrical energy is consumed.

P = VI = I²R = V²/R

Unit: Watt (W). Commercial unit of electrical energy is Kilowatt-hour (kWh).
1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules (1 Unit of electricity).
