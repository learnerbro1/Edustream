Heredity

CBSE Class 10 • Biology — Chapter 8 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Introduction to Heredity

Heredity: The transmission of characters/traits from parents to their offspring.

Variations: Differences in traits shown by individuals of a species. They are necessary for survival and evolution.

2. Mendel's Work on Pea Plants

Gregor Johann Mendel is known as the Father of Genetics. He chose garden peas (Pisum sativum) because they have distinct contrasting characters, short life spans, and are self-pollinating.

Monohybrid Cross

Cross considering only one pair of contrasting characters (e.g., Tall x Dwarf).

Fig 8.1: Monohybrid cross showing inheritance of height (Tall T and dwarf t)
F1 Generation: All Tall (Tt)
F2 Phenotypic Ratio: 3:1 (Tall:Dwarf)
F2 Genotypic Ratio: 1:2:1 (TT:Tt:tt)

Conclusion: Traits can be dominant (expresses itself in F1) or recessive (remains hidden in F1, expresses in F2).

Dihybrid Cross

Cross considering two pairs of contrasting characters (e.g., Round Yellow x Wrinkled Green seeds).

F2 Phenotypic Ratio: 9:3:3:1

Conclusion (Law of Independent Assortment): Traits are inherited independently of each other.

3. Sex Determination in Humans

Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes. 22 pairs are autosomes, and the 23rd pair are sex chromosomes.

Female: XX sex chromosomes. Produces only one type of gamete (X).
Male: XY sex chromosomes. Produces two types of gametes (50% X and 50% Y).

If a sperm carrying an X chromosome fertilizes the egg, it results in a girl (XX). If a sperm carrying a Y chromosome fertilizes the egg, it results in a boy (XY).
The father determines the sex of the child.

Fig 8.2: Flowchart showing sex determination in human beings

★ Quick memory tips

Gene: A specific section of DNA that provides information for one protein.
Alleles: Different versions of the same gene (e.g., T and t).
