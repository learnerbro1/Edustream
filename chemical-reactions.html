Chemical Reactions & Equations

CBSE Class 10 • Chemistry — Chapter 1 • Quick Revision Sheet + PYQs

1. Basics of Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction is a process in which new substances with new chemical properties are formed.

  • Reactants: Substances that take part in the chemical reaction.
  • Products: New substances formed as a result of the chemical reaction.

Indicators of a chemical reaction: Change in state, Change in colour, Evolution of a gas, Change in temperature.

Law of Conservation of Mass:
Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
(Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products. Number of atoms of each element remains the same.)

2. Types of Chemical Reactions

A. Combination Reaction

Two or more reactants combine to form a single product. (A + B → AB)

  • Example (Quicklime to Slaked lime): CaO(s) + H&sub2;O(l) → Ca(OH)&sub2;(aq) + Heat
  • Burning of coal: C(s) + O&sub2;(g) → CO&sub2;(g)

Exothermic Reactions: Reactions in which heat is released along with the formation of products (e.g., Respiration, burning of natural gas).

B. Decomposition Reaction

A single reactant breaks down to give simpler products. (AB → A + B). It is usually Endothermic (requires energy).

Fig 1.1: Electrolysis of water (Electrical Decomposition)
Thermal Decomposition: Heating is used.
CaCO&sub3;(s) → CaO(s) + CO&sub2;(g)
Photochemical Decomposition: Sunlight is used.
2AgCl(s) → 2Ag(s) + Cl&sub2;(g)
(Used in black & white photography)

C. Displacement Reaction

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its salt solution.

Example: Fe(s) + CuSO&sub4;(aq) → FeSO&sub4;(aq) + Cu(s)
Observation: The blue colour of copper sulphate fades to pale green, and a brown deposit forms on the iron nail.

Fig 1.2: Displacement reaction between Iron and Copper Sulphate

D. Double Displacement Reaction

An exchange of ions occurs between two compounds.

Example: Na&sub2;SO&sub4;(aq) + BaCl&sub2;(aq) → BaSO&sub4;(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
Observation: A white precipitate of Barium Sulphate is formed. Such reactions are also called Precipitation Reactions.

3. Oxidation and Reduction (Redox)

Oxidation: Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.
2Cu + O&sub2; → 2CuO (Copper is oxidized)
Reduction: Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.
CuO + H&sub2; → Cu + H&sub2;O (CuO is reduced)

Redox Reaction: When oxidation and reduction take place simultaneously.

4. Effects of Oxidation in Everyday Life

  • Corrosion: Slow degradation of metals by the action of air, moisture, or chemicals (e.g., Rusting of iron, black coating on silver).
  • Rancidity: Oxidation of fats and oils in food, changing its smell and taste.
    Prevention: Adding antioxidants, flushing bags of chips with nitrogen gas, keeping food in air-tight containers.

★ Quick memory tips

  • Magnesium ribbon is cleaned before burning to remove the protective layer of Magnesium Oxide.
  • Exothermic = Heat Exits. Endothermic = Heat Enters.
  • For balancing equations: Always start with the atom present in the maximum number.
